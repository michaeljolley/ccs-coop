Part 1: Responsible AI & What's Next (~25 minutes)

You now have a superpower

You can describe a problem in plain English and produce working software. That's genuinely powerful. But with power comes responsibility. Let's talk about the edges.

Things to keep in mind

1. AI amplifies you, for good or bad

Copilot makes you faster at building. But "faster" isn't always "better." Take time to think about:

  • Does this app respect user privacy?
  • Could this be used to harm someone?
  • Am I being honest about what my app does?

2. AI output isn't always original

LLMs learned from human-written code. The code they generate might resemble someone else's work. When building for real:

  • Add your own creativity and ideas on top
  • Don't claim AI-generated code as entirely your own intellectual creation
  • Use open-source licenses when sharing your projects

3. AI can perpetuate bias

If the training data contained biases (it did), the model can reproduce them. Be aware when building apps that:

  • Make recommendations about people
  • Use categories or labels for humans
  • Affect access to resources or opportunities

4. Don't outsource your judgment

The most important lesson: you are the human in the loop. AI is a tool. You decide what to build, whether the output is good, and whether it should exist. Never stop thinking critically just because a machine produced something.

💡 The simplest ethical test Before shipping something: "Would I be comfortable if my name was permanently attached to this, and everyone could see it?" If yes, ship it. If not, rethink.

Where to go from here

You've completed this course. Here's what you can do next:

  • Keep building: the best way to get better is to build things you care about
  • Learn more JavaScript: javascript.info is excellent and free
  • Try React or a framework: once you're comfortable with JS, frameworks make bigger apps manageable
  • Build an MCP server: take a data source you use and make it accessible to AI
  • Contribute to open source: find a project on GitHub, use Copilot to help you submit your first PR
  • Take AP Computer Science: if your school offers it, you now have a massive head start

What's the most important responsibility when using AI to build software?

Always writing prompts longer than 50 words Never sharing code that AI helped generate Making sure the AI never makes a mistake Maintaining your own critical judgment about output

Part 2: Your Wildcard Project (~35 minutes)

This is YOUR project. No prompts given, no instructions to follow. You pick the idea, you describe it to Copilot, you build it, you deploy it.

The process (you know this now)

  1. Define what you're building: one sentence
  2. Write a detailed prompt: specific behavior, format, constraints
  3. Generate and test: save, open in browser, verify
  4. Iterate: fix bugs, add features, polish
  5. Deploy: push to GitHub Pages, share the URL

Need ideas?

  • A recipe scaler (input servings, adjust quantities)
  • A workout/gym tracker
  • A playlist shuffler or music discovery tool
  • A GPA calculator
  • A collaborative drawing canvas
  • A typing speed test
  • A meme generator
  • A countdown to graduation/summer/event
  • A "would you rather" game for friends
  • Something completely different. Surprise us!
